Mainland China

    All travel notes

    Visa & entry rules

    Check the rules for your passport, not someone else's. Visa-free policies depend on nationality, purpose and length of stay.

    • Check the official Chinese embassy or National Immigration Administration website for your exact passport.
    • Keep your onward ticket, hotel details and return itinerary easy to access.

    Entry rules can change; verify again shortly before departure.

    Passport & document backup

    Keep your identity documents usable even if your phone battery dies.

    • Check that your passport meets entry and airline requirements.
    • Save an offline copy of your passport information page and insurance details.

    Keep originals secure; a photo does not replace a required original.

    Internet & overseas apps

    Some websites and international apps may not work normally on mainland Chinese networks.

    • Decide whether to use international roaming, an eligible travel eSIM or local mobile service.
    • Download offline maps, contacts and essential information before flying.

    Check current access rules; unauthorized VPN services can carry legal risks.

    SIM card, eSIM or roaming?

    The easiest connectivity option depends on your phone and your trip.

    • Check whether your handset supports eSIM and whether your plan covers mainland China.
    • Compare activation steps, data limits, costs and number availability.

    Some China-only services may need a local mobile number for verification.

    Set up mobile payments

    QR payments are extremely common in shops, restaurants and taxis.

    • Install official Alipay or WeChat apps and explore international card setup before your trip.
    • Keep a second payment method and some RMB cash as a fallback.

    Your bank may block an unfamiliar transaction; make sure you can reach it.

    The China app starter kit

    A few well-chosen apps can replace hours of guesswork.

    • Prepare maps, translation, payment and transport apps in advance.
    • Learn how to copy Chinese addresses, not just English place names.

    Link to HaoEasy for step-by-step app walkthroughs.

    Power plugs & charging

    Don't land with the wrong plug or a charger that cannot handle local power.

    • Mainland China commonly uses 220V/50Hz electricity and several plug shapes; pack a suitable adapter.
    • Check the voltage rating on hair tools or other appliances before using them.

    Most modern phone and laptop chargers support 100–240V, but verify yours.

    Power banks on domestic flights

    A useful power bank can be stopped at security if it lacks the required marking.

    • Check the clear, valid CCC/3C marking if you plan to fly within mainland China.
    • Keep power banks in carry-on baggage and confirm airline capacity rules.

    Domestic flights restrict power banks without clear valid 3C marks or in recalled batches.

    Medication & travel insurance

    Plan for needs that are harder to solve in another language.

    • Carry medications in their original packaging with any relevant prescription documentation.
    • Choose insurance that fits your medical care, activities and destinations.

    Check restrictions for controlled medicines before travel.

    What to pack beyond clothes

    Small everyday items make the first 24 hours much easier.

    • Pack tissues, hand sanitizer, a reusable bottle, appropriate shoes and your charging adapter.
    • Keep a lightweight layer, weather protection and offline hotel address handy.

    Weather can vary dramatically across China; check your specific city and dates.

    Immigration: what to expect

    Arrival can involve passport checks and questions about your visit.

    • Keep your destination address, return/onward plans and any required documents accessible.
    • Follow official signs and instructions; allow time before making tight connections.

    Requirements vary by route, passport and current immigration policy.

    Your first hour after landing

    A calm arrival is often just a sequence of small decisions.

    • Connect to your prepared mobile service and confirm your hotel address.
    • Choose an authorized taxi stand, airport train or transit route before leaving the terminal.

    Avoid accepting unsolicited private rides inside the arrivals hall.

    Airport Wi-Fi & verification

    Free Wi-Fi is useful, but joining a network can sometimes need extra steps.

    • Look for official airport network details rather than unknown hotspots.
    • Have an alternative connection if a phone-number verification step does not work.

    Never enter card details into suspicious login pages.

    Getting cash in RMB

    Even if you pay by phone most of the time, a backup matters.

    • Use bank ATMs or authorized exchange counters to get modest RMB cash.
    • Check exchange and withdrawal charges with your card issuer.

    Keep some smaller denominations for situations where making change is difficult.

    Your first QR payment

    The QR code you see at a counter is not always the same type.

    • A merchant may ask you to scan their code or show your payment code to be scanned.
    • Confirm the displayed amount before approving payment.

    Use only trusted payment apps and check the merchant name.

    Finding a legitimate taxi

    The easiest ride is usually the one booked through an official channel.

    • Follow airport taxi signs or arrange a ride through a reputable app.
    • Match your vehicle and plate to your booking details.

    Save your hotel's name and address in Chinese.

    Hotel check-in with a passport

    Your hotel will usually register your accommodation details.

    • Keep your passport and booking confirmation easy to reach.
    • Check the property's policy on deposits, check-in times and foreign-card payments.

    Don't assume every staff member speaks English; show the reservation screen.

    Your first meal without Mandarin

    You can order a good meal without knowing every character.

    • Choose a place with photographs or use your camera translation tool on a menu.
    • Show a clear Chinese dish name and quantity if needed.

    For allergies, use a carefully translated written card and confirm with staff.

    Squat toilets & seated toilets

    Many public restrooms offer squat toilets, although seated toilets are common in hotels and modern facilities.

    • Look at stall icons to choose the type you prefer.
    • Be ready to use a squat toilet at transport hubs or older attractions.

    Carry tissues and sanitizer; availability varies by facility.

    Always bring some tissues

    Toilet paper is not guaranteed in every public restroom.

    • Keep a compact pack in your day bag.
    • Check whether a dispenser is outside the cubicles before entering.

    Use the posted instructions for where used paper belongs.

    Can toilet paper be flushed?

    Restroom plumbing and disposal practices vary.

    • Look for a sign or bin indicating the facility's paper-disposal procedure.
    • When unsure, follow the posted local instruction rather than assuming.

    Avoid flushing wipes, sanitary items or other non-toilet-paper products.

    Can I drink tap water?

    Travelers generally should not drink untreated tap water in mainland China.

    • Choose sealed bottled water or properly boiled/filtered drinking water.
    • Look for designated drinking-water dispensers in hotels and stations.

    A kettle is not proof the tap water itself is drinkable without treatment.

    Why is hot water everywhere?

    Hot and warm drinking water is common in homes, stations and restaurants.

    • Ask for hot water if you want it, or request cold water specifically.
    • Let very hot water cool before drinking.

    A restaurant may offer tea or warm water rather than ice water.

    QR codes in daily life

    QR codes may handle payment, menus, queues, tickets and service requests.

    • Check whether the code opens a menu, a mini-program or a payment screen.
    • If a code does not load, ask staff for a manual alternative.

    Avoid scanning random codes posted over official signs.

    Shared power banks

    Shopping malls and restaurants may offer rentable charging batteries.

    • Check the return locations and charging fee before renting.
    • Confirm the app works with your payment setup.

    Return the device properly; leaving without checking out may keep fees running.

    Public trash bins & recycling

    Waste sorting labels can be different from home.

    • Look for the posted categories before throwing items away.
    • Carry small rubbish until you find a bin.

    Follow local rules rather than guessing from bin color alone.

    Crowded places & escalators

    Large cities move quickly, especially near metros.

    • Keep bags close to you and check signs for queues and train exits.
    • Avoid stopping abruptly at the top of escalators or in station passageways.

    Give yourself extra time at major hubs and during holidays.

    Public restrooms: useful signs

    Recognizing a few symbols saves time during a busy day.

    • Look for 男 (men), 女 (women), 洗手间 (restroom) and 无障碍 (accessible).
    • Check for accessible facilities where available.

    Don't assume every restroom has an English label.

    How to order from a QR menu

    Some restaurants ask customers to scan a table code before ordering.

    • Scan with the app indicated by the restaurant and check table number if shown.
    • Review quantities, spice level and extras before placing the order.

    Ask staff to help when the menu does not load on your phone.

    Menus without English

    Photos, Chinese names and translations together work best.

    • Use your camera's translation feature for descriptions.
    • Point to a dish photo and confirm the number of portions.

    Automatic translation may miss ingredients and cooking methods.

    Sharing dishes, Chinese-style

    Many meals are designed for a group rather than one plate per person.

    • Order several dishes for the table if you're eating with others.
    • Use serving utensils when offered.

    Portion sizes vary; begin with a few dishes and add more if needed.

    Eating with food allergies

    Written communication matters when an ingredient can be dangerous.

    • Prepare a professionally checked Chinese allergy card that names the ingredient clearly.
    • Show it to staff before ordering and ask about sauces and cooking oils.

    If you have a severe allergy, do not rely on machine translation alone.

    Is all Chinese food spicy?

    Chinese cuisine varies enormously by region.

    • Say 不要辣 (bú yào là) if you would like no chili.
    • Ask about sauces, peppercorns and chili oil separately when relevant.

    Some dishes contain spice in their prepared base; check before ordering.

    Hotpot for first-timers

    Hotpot is interactive, social and easy to customize once you know the basics.

    • Choose a broth and a variety of ingredients before cooking at the table.
    • Keep raw and cooked utensils separate and cook ingredients thoroughly.

    Ask for a mild or divided pot if you're unsure about spice.

    Breakfast like a local

    Breakfast is a fast route into everyday Chinese food culture.

    • Try regional favorites such as baozi, congee, soy milk or noodles.
    • Watch whether the shop uses counter ordering or mobile payment.

    Breakfast shops may close earlier than restaurants.

    Takeout & pickup shelves

    Delivery is popular but can be harder without a local contact number.

    • Check whether your app supports your phone number and payment method.
    • Set an accurate pickup point, especially for large buildings.

    Some residential buildings use delivery cabinets or gate collection.

    How to find a good restaurant

    Maps and local review apps show different kinds of information.

    • Search the dish name or cuisine on Dianping or a local map app.
    • Read recent reviews and check the exact address and current hours.

    A busy tourist area does not always mean the best value.

    High-speed rail basics

    China's high-speed rail is one of the simplest ways to travel between major cities.

    • Check the exact train number, departure station and boarding time.
    • Arrive with enough time for security and passport verification.

    Station names that sound similar may be far apart.

    Train tickets with a passport

    International visitors can book trains with accepted travel documents.

    • Use the official 12306 service or a reputable ticket agent and enter names carefully.
    • Bring the same original passport used for booking.

    Ticketing, verification and refunds follow specific rules; review them in advance.

    Know your railway station

    Beijing South and Beijing West, for example, are not interchangeable.

    • Copy the full Chinese name of the departure station.
    • Check travel time from your hotel before choosing a train.

    Changing to the wrong station can ruin a tight schedule.

    Station security & boarding

    Expect security checks at many railway stations and transport hubs.

    • Keep prohibited items out of your luggage and have documents accessible.
    • Follow the boarding gate and train number on station screens.

    Different stations may use different verification methods.

    Taking the metro

    Metro is often easier than dealing with road traffic.

    • Check station names, line numbers and exit letters in advance.
    • Learn how ticket machines, fare cards or transit QR codes work in that city.

    Services and payment methods differ between cities.

    How to order a ride-hailing car

    Pickup points can be tricky in busy neighborhoods.

    • Set a precise pickup location and check the vehicle plate number.
    • Keep your Chinese destination name handy.

    Avoid following a driver to an unapproved pickup area.

    Chinese addresses that work

    A good address needs more than a business's English name.

    • Copy the Chinese venue name and street address into maps.
    • Save the location pin, not just a screenshot of the English name.

    Large malls may have multiple entrances and exits.

    Shared bikes & e-bikes

    Cycling is popular, but road rules and rental access vary.

    • Check whether the rental app accepts your account and payment method.
    • Use designated parking areas and wear appropriate safety gear.

    Do not assume shared e-bikes are available or legal everywhere.

    Airport transfers made easy

    Choose a transfer based on luggage, arrival time and distance.

    • Compare airport rail, metro and official taxi routes.
    • Check late-night service limits before landing.

    International and domestic terminals may use different pickup points.

    Choosing where to stay

    The right neighborhood can save more time than a cheaper hotel rate.

    • Pick an area close to a metro line and your planned sights.
    • Compare travel time to the airport and railway station.

    Map distances may not capture river crossings or station exits.

    Can foreigners stay in hotels?

    Hotels must handle foreign guest accommodation registration; blanket claims that foreigners can only stay in special hotels are misleading.

    • Confirm your booking and bring your passport.
    • Contact the property beforehand if its booking process is unclear.

    A hotel may have operational limitations; confirm specific arrangements.

    Checking in and deposits

    A reservation does not always cover incidentals or local deposits.

    • Keep your booking reference and passport available.
    • Ask whether deposits are preauthorized, refundable and accepted by your card.

    Save a photo of the booking terms before arrival.

    What is in a Chinese hotel room?

    Amenities vary by budget, chain and location.

    • Check whether towels, drinking water, Wi-Fi and laundry are included.
    • Bring personal toiletries if you have strong preferences.

    Do not assume a kettle makes unboiled tap water safe to drink.

    Apartments and private stays

    Non-hotel accommodation may require extra registration steps.

    • Ask your host what process applies to your address.
    • Complete the legally required accommodation registration within the applicable timeframe.

    Online registration options have expanded; check local official instructions.

    Hotels without English-speaking staff

    A simple bilingual note solves most requests.

    • Save check-in date, booking number and room type in screenshots.
    • Use short written sentences or a translation app at the front desk.

    For urgent issues, ask the accommodation to help contact appropriate services.

    Picking a booking platform

    The best app depends on language, inventory, fees and customer support.

    • Compare Trip.com, Ctrip and other reputable platforms for the same dates.
    • Check the final total, cancellation terms and location carefully.

    For major holidays, reconfirm your stay close to arrival.

    Alipay or WeChat Pay?

    Both are widely used; having a backup can be helpful.

    • Try linking an eligible international bank card using the app's official flow.
    • Test a low-value payment when possible before a critical journey.

    Acceptance and identity verification vary by bank, account and merchant.

    Can I still pay with cash?

    RMB cash remains a legal payment method in China.

    • Carry some small notes as a contingency.
    • Check your change and price before paying.

    Small merchants may have limited change despite cash acceptance rules.

    What if the QR payment fails?

    A failed scan is frustrating but often solvable.

    • Check connectivity, payment method and whether the merchant needs a different code.
    • Ask to try the other supported payment method or pay with cash.

    Avoid repeatedly approving pending charges without checking account history.

    International bank cards

    Card acceptance is less uniform than QR payments.

    • Look for accepted network logos and ask before ordering or paying.
    • Know your bank's travel-alert and fraud-help options.

    Some smaller businesses may not accept foreign cards directly.

    Chinese mini-programs

    Many services run inside WeChat or Alipay rather than a separate app.

    • Open mini-programs only from verified business codes or search results.
    • Review requested permissions and payment terms.

    Some functions may require a mainland phone number or identity check.

    Refunds and returns

    Return policies vary by seller, product and platform.

    • Keep your receipt, order number and payment record.
    • Check whether refunds return to the original payment method.

    Cross-border card refunds may take time to appear.

    Bargaining or fixed prices?

    Most malls and formal stores use fixed prices; some markets may negotiate.

    • Ask the price clearly before agreeing.
    • Be polite if you choose not to buy.

    Don't pressure small vendors or assume every market item is negotiable.

    Supermarkets & self-checkout

    Some products are pre-priced while fresh produce may need weighing.

    • Look for self-checkout signs, basket scanners and staff assistance.
    • Check whether fruits or vegetables need a printed weighing label.

    Save your payment screen in case the exit gate asks for proof.

    Tipping in China

    Tipping is generally not expected in routine restaurants and taxis.

    • Pay the listed price unless a service charge is shown.
    • Check a private tour's policies if you are unsure.

    Avoid assuming foreign tipping customs apply everywhere.

    Greetings & politeness

    A few simple Chinese words make interactions warmer.

    • Start with 你好 (nǐ hǎo) or 请问 (qǐng wèn).
    • Say 谢谢 (xiè xie) when someone helps you.

    Speak slowly and show the written Chinese place name if needed.

    Queues & personal space

    Crowding can vary by venue and time of day.

    • Follow queue rails, floor markings and station instructions.
    • Be patient around popular attractions and transport gates.

    Try not to block people taking photos or entering trains.

    Taking photos respectfully

    Photo rules differ between temples, museums and other venues.

    • Check signs before photographing exhibitions or security areas.
    • Ask before taking close-up portraits of strangers.

    Some locations prohibit tripods, flash or commercial filming.

    Temple visiting etiquette

    Temples are active spiritual places, not just attractions.

    • Keep voices low and follow posted photography restrictions.
    • Dress and behave respectfully near ceremonies and worshippers.

    Rules differ by religious site; take cues from on-site guidance.

    Giving gifts in China

    Thoughtful gifts can be appreciated in social settings.

    • Choose something modest and appropriate for the relationship.
    • Present gifts politely and avoid assuming every tradition is universal.

    Customs vary by family, age and region.

    Chinese holidays & crowds

    Travel patterns change dramatically over major holidays.

    • Check dates for Lunar New Year, May Day and National Day.
    • Book popular transport and lodging earlier during peak periods.

    Some smaller businesses may have different hours during holidays.

    Local customs vary by city

    China is diverse; what feels typical in one city may differ elsewhere.

    • Explore local cuisine, dialect and etiquette with an open mind.
    • Ask for help when a local system is unfamiliar.

    Avoid treating one travel story as a rule for the whole country.

    Emergency numbers to save

    Know who to call before you need urgent help.

    • Police: 110. Fire: 119. Ambulance: 120.
    • Save your hotel's address in Chinese to share with responders.

    Call local emergency services for immediate danger, not an online guide.

    If you lose your passport

    A lost passport is a travel disruption that needs fast official help.

    • Report the loss to local police as appropriate and contact your embassy or consulate.
    • Ask your airline and immigration authorities what replacement documents are required.

    Allow extra time; replacement travel papers may affect departure.

    If you lose your phone

    Your phone may hold payment, maps and authentication access.

    • Use account security features to lock or locate your device when possible.
    • Contact your mobile carrier and financial providers if needed.

    Keep a backup list of emergency numbers and hotel details offline.

    Missing your train

    A missed train does not always mean the ticket is usable on another service.

    • Ask station staff or check the booking platform for current change rules.
    • Keep your passport and booking details ready.

    Rules depend on timing, fare and availability; don't assume a free rebooking.

    Feeling unwell abroad

    Find medical care early for serious symptoms.

    • Ask your hotel or travel insurer for a nearby appropriate hospital.
    • Bring identification, insurance details and any medication information.

    For emergencies, call 120.

    Taxi or payment disputes

    Staying calm and keeping a record is more helpful than arguing.

    • Save the ride receipt, payment record and vehicle details.
    • Use the platform's in-app customer service where applicable.

    For safety threats or suspected crime, contact local police.

    Food allergy emergency

    Serious allergic reactions need immediate medical attention.

    • Carry prescribed emergency medication and a bilingual allergy card.
    • Call 120 and communicate the allergen clearly if an emergency occurs.

    Do not rely on a general travel article for medical decisions.

    Asking for help in Chinese

    A short readable sentence can be enough.

    • Show 可以帮我一下吗？ (Can you help me?) on your phone.
    • Point to a map or address to clarify what you need.

    For a serious emergency, seek direct human help promptly.

    Travel outside peak hours

    Small timing changes can transform popular sights.

    • Visit high-traffic attractions early when permitted.
    • Choose weekday visits for flexible destinations when possible.

    Check official hours before building a route.

    Book popular attractions in advance

    Ticketing is not standardized across China.

    • Check official attraction websites and verified booking channels.
    • Note whether a passport number or time slot is required.

    Be wary of unofficial accounts promising guaranteed entry.

    Avoid common travel scams

    A friendly invitation is not proof a business is trustworthy.

    • Agree on prices and services before committing.
    • Choose official ticket sellers, ride services and reputable payment screens.

    If you feel pressured, leave and seek assistance.

    Find restaurants locals use

    Local-search tools help, but context matters.

    • Compare recent photos, menu prices and review trends.
    • Search with Chinese dish names for more precise results.

    Don't rely on a star rating alone.

    Save destinations the Chinese way

    A Chinese map pin is more useful than an English-only itinerary.

    • Save Chinese names of your hotel, landmarks and restaurants.
    • Copy the full address into your map or ride-hailing app.

    Check the exact branch when businesses have several locations.

    Watch changing opening hours

    Tourist spots may close on certain weekdays or holidays.

    • Check official opening times the night before.
    • Keep one nearby backup activity for weather or closures.

    Museum schedules and special exhibitions can change.

    Travel light between cities

    Dragging luggage through busy stations can spoil a travel day.

    • Check hotel luggage storage and station facilities in advance.
    • Choose a bag you can lift into train racks.

    Only use reputable luggage storage services.

    Use offline backups wisely

    Don't make your whole trip depend on one app.

    • Save screenshots of bookings, addresses and key phrases offline.
    • Carry a small card with your hotel's Chinese address.

    Protect sensitive documents and delete unneeded copies afterward.

    Five-minute language survival

    You don't need fluency to feel more confident.

    • Learn hello, thank you, where is, how much and help me.
    • Save Chinese phrases you can show without speaking.

    HaoTalk can turn these into short, useful lessons.

    Hong Kong entry & border checks

    Hong Kong has its own immigration requirements, separate from those for mainland China.

    • Check the Hong Kong Immigration Department rules for your passport and length of visit.
    • If you will also enter mainland China, verify those entry rules separately.

    Travel between Hong Kong and mainland China involves border checks; do not assume the same visa waiver applies.

    Octopus: your handy travel card

    The Octopus card makes everyday travel and small purchases easier in Hong Kong.

    • Check where to buy or use a physical or mobile Octopus card.
    • Use it for eligible MTR, bus, ferry and everyday payments; check each operator.

    Keep a backup payment method; not every merchant accepts the same options.

    Currency & payments

    Hong Kong uses the Hong Kong dollar (HKD), not the renminbi as its primary currency.

    • Plan for some HKD and an international bank card.
    • Check exchange rates and foreign transaction fees before using your card.

    Mainland QR payment habits do not always carry over; card and Octopus acceptance is common.

    Type G plugs & charging

    Hong Kong commonly uses British-style three-prong (Type G) power outlets.

    • Bring a suitable Type G plug adapter.
    • Check the voltage rating on your devices before plugging them in.

    Hong Kong mains electricity is generally 220V/50Hz; check your appliance label.

    MTR, ferries, trams & buses

    Hong Kong has an extensive public transport network, with different experiences above and below ground.

    • Plan journeys using the MTR or an up-to-date journey planner.
    • Try a Star Ferry crossing or a Hong Kong tram if it fits your route.

    Schedules, ticket types and interchange arrangements can change.

    Cantonese & English

    Cantonese is the main spoken language; English is widely used in visitor-facing contexts.

    • Save hotel and attraction names in Chinese and English.
    • Use clear, simple requests or translation tools when needed.

    Written traditional Chinese is common in Hong Kong, unlike simplified Chinese commonly seen on the mainland.

    Getting between Hong Kong & Shenzhen

    Crossing the border is a journey in its own right.

    • Check the exact boundary crossing opening hours and entry eligibility.
    • Allow time for immigration and customs on both sides.

    Some rail and road crossings have separate processes and operating hours.

    Restrooms & daily etiquette

    Hong Kong day-to-day travel can feel quite different from a mainland city.

    • Public restroom facilities vary, so carry a small pack of tissues.
    • Be mindful of local queuing and transport etiquette.

    Do not automatically apply mainland-only app, connectivity or payment advice to Hong Kong.
